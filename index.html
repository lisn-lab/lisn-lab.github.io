Our research

Inner speech, mind, and body

We study how silent language links thought, perception, and physical action – from why we talk to ourselves when facing uncertainty to how words draw on bodily experience and social context. We also explore how inner speech varies across people, and what changes when it becomes repetitive, intrusive, or heard as a voice.
Inner speech and thinking Why do we talk to ourselves in silence, and how does it serve mind and body? How inner speech arises under uncertainty, shifts form across tasks, coordinates brain and bodily interactions, and varies from person to person. How word meaning shifts with bodily experience, emotion, culture, and everyday context – and how embodied human understanding compares with AI. ExperimentsEye trackingEEG Explore → Voice hearing and rumination What changes when inner speech becomes repetitive, intrusive, or heard as a voice? How expectations shape voice hearing, why negative inner speech can get stuck in loops, and how brain and bodily signals can help detect both. How we study it

Experimental psychology Lab, online, and eye-tracking experiments testing how inner speech shapes perception, attention, and action.
Language and thought analysis Analysing what people say and think aloud to trace how self-directed language shifts in everyday problem solving.
Brain and body measurement Recording brain activity (EEG) alongside subtle tongue, eye, and bodily signals as thoughts unfold.
AI and computational modelling Detecting inner speech from brain and bodily signals, and building self-talking models to understand how minds navigate uncertainty.
