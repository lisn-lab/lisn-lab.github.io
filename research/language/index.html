Research

Embodied and situated language

How do our bodies, senses, and surroundings shape what words mean and how we act on them?

ExperimentsEye trackingEEG

Words do not carry fixed dictionary meanings. What a word means – and how it shapes what we feel or do – shifts with our bodies, senses, emotions, and cultural surroundings. We study how that grounding works for concrete objects and for abstract ideas such as trust or love. Our research shows that size acts as a core organising principle across concepts – much like the familiar idea that “size matters”, it helps set the scale and intensity of our judgements and actions – while emotion, imagery, and culture shape how words are understood across first and second languages.

The same embodied process operates when we direct language at ourselves in inner speech, linking silent words with movement and perception – and raising sharp questions about how human understanding differs from AI language models. We study meaning-making in real-world settings such as reading and classrooms. Using eye tracking to see how readers sample upcoming words in context, we study how the mind predicts meaning on the fly and build adaptive digital text displays that respond smoothly to a reader’s gaze.

Current projects

Size and meaning How size acts as an organising principle across concrete and abstract concepts, shaping the scale of judgements and actions, with collaborators at Purdue University.
Culture and emotion in a second language How cultural context changes the emotional force of words in first and second languages. Yanxi Lu
Grounding abstract concepts How abstract ideas such as trust or freedom draw on sensory experience and personal life memories. Guyu Shen
Metaphor and multisensory meaning How visual metaphor and concreteness shape interpretation. Jiaqi Wang
Language structure and perspective How differences in grammar between Mandarin and English shape mental perspective during comprehension. Jiaxuan Chen
Adaptive reading Using eye tracking to study how readers sample and infer meaning in context, and building adaptive digital text displays that respond to gaze, with the University of Glasgow. Abigail Petrsoric
Noise, inner speech and learning How classroom noise affects the development of inner speech and cognition. El Smith
