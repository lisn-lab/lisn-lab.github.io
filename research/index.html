Research

Inner speech, mind, and body

Our research asks why humans talk to themselves silently, how this inner speech connects brain, body, and senses to guide what we perceive and do, and how it differs across individuals. By studying both everyday thinking and lived experiences such as voice hearing and rumination, we aim to build a unified science of inner language.

Our guiding framework

Linguistic active inference

We propose that inner speech helps the mind and body deal with uncertainty. When a sight, sound, or decision is unclear, putting thoughts into words distils messy sensory detail into manageable ideas and turns broad goals into concrete actions. What we say to ourselves – from a single silent word to a full sentence or internal conversation – shapes what we expect to perceive and how firmly we act on it. This framework, called Linguistic Active Inference Theory, links the many forms and uses of inner speech and generates testable predictions for our experiments and computational models.

How we study it

Experimental psychology Lab, online, and eye-tracking experiments testing how inner speech shapes perception, attention, and action.
Language and thought analysis Analysing what people say and think aloud to trace how self-directed language shifts in everyday problem solving.
Brain and body measurement Recording brain activity (EEG) alongside subtle tongue, eye, and bodily signals as thoughts unfold.
AI and computational modelling Detecting inner speech from brain and bodily signals, and building self-talking models to understand how minds navigate uncertainty.
