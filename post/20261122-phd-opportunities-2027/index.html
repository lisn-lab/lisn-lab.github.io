PhD Opportunities for October 2027 Entry

Bo Yao
2026-11-22 6 min read

We are welcoming enquiries from prospective PhD students for October 2027 entry in the Department of Psychology at Lancaster University. Below are three flagship project directions listed on the Lancaster Psychology PhD opportunities page. We also welcome original proposals connected to inner speech, mind, and body.

1. Talking to ourselves or talking to AI? Inner speech and human-AI thinking

Language is a tool for thought. Putting a problem into words can hold goals in mind, structure plans, and expose flaws in reasoning, much of it through inner speech (Clark, 1998; Alderson-Day & Fernyhough, 2015). Many people now articulate their problems to large language models (LLMs) instead. If the model generates and evaluates the answers, the user may bypass the reasoning and retain only its output. In a field experiment, students with unrestricted GPT-4 access performed better on practice problems yet worse on a later exam taken without it, and did not perceive the loss (Bastani et al., 2025).

An LLM may also act as a dialogic partner. Inner speech often takes the form of a dialogue in which one perspective questions another (Fernyhough, 1996), and people vary in how dialogic their inner speech is (McCarthy-Jones & Fernyhough, 2011). For those who rarely debate with themselves, an LLM could supply the missing interlocutor. Creative thinking shows what is at stake. Ideas from an LLM made individual stories more creative while making stories more similar to one another (Doshi & Hauser, 2024).

This PhD will explore the practices and consequences of human-AI co-cognition. When does dialogue with an LLM scaffold reasoning, and when does it replace it? Does habitual LLM use change how often people talk to themselves and how dialogic that self-talk is? What does it do to brain health and neurocognitive fitness? You will combine reasoning and creativity tasks performed with and without AI, recordings of self-talk and prompts, inner speech questionnaires, and EEG.

  • Official listing: Lancaster Psychology – Talking to ourselves or talking to AI?

2. Why do we talk to ourselves? Inner speech, uncertainty, and individual differences

Imagine working through a puzzle and silently asking yourself, “What should I try next?” Why put that question into words? What makes us begin talking to ourselves, and how might those words help us find a way forward? Why might someone else approach the same puzzle with little inner speech? This PhD explores a fundamental question about the relationship between language and thought – when and why we recruit an inner voice, and how this varies from person to person.

You will investigate the possibility that certain types of uncertainty prompt us to think in words. Does inner speech emerge when we are unsure how to interpret a situation, choose between alternatives, or decide what to do next? Does this depend on how useful language is for the task? Through behavioural experiments, EEG, and computational modelling, you will examine how people’s reported inner speech changes as they encounter uncertainty and learn how to respond, and how these patterns differ between individuals.

Individual differences will be central to testing this explanation. Does uncertainty prompt inner speech to the same extent in people who report frequent verbal thought, vivid visual imagery, or little of either? What strategies do people use when they experience little inner speech? You could also explore how neurodivergence relates to these patterns, or investigate what talking to ourselves changes in our decisions.

The aim is to develop and test an explanation of when and why people recruit language to guide their thinking, and how this varies between individuals. You will build a programme of experiments and an original dataset, using these to evaluate a model that makes explicit predictions about when inner speech should occur and how its use should vary from person to person. Along the way, you will develop the experimental and computational skills to pursue your own questions about the mind.

  • Official listing: Lancaster Psychology – Why do we talk to ourselves?

3. Tracking mental health vulnerabilities through language prediction failures in adolescence

The brain is a prediction engine that uses language to anticipate perceptions, guide actions, and construct identity. During adolescence, this system confronts major challenges as the brain rewires itself whilst navigating intensified social pressures, information overload, and identity formation. When linguistic prediction falters, mental health vulnerabilities can emerge – intrusive thoughts feel alien, rumination spirals, and social signals become harder to read.

This PhD investigates fundamental mechanisms in youth mental wellbeing, focusing on identifying subtle signatures of when the brain’s linguistic prediction system begins to falter. You will work primarily with healthy young people experiencing everyday adolescent stress and self-doubt, using methods spanning phenomenological interviews, computational behavioural tasks, and neurophysiological recordings.

Your work will establish foundational science mapping how variation in these mechanisms relates to different experiences of distress, worry, and self-perception across everyday adolescence and at-risk populations. If you are fascinated by the intersection of language, prediction, and mental experience, this project offers the opportunity to build theoretical frameworks, empirical evidence, and tools that advance how we understand the mental health spectrum and vulnerability.

  • Official listing: Lancaster Psychology – Tracking mental health vulnerabilities

Funding routes and how to apply

Prospective students for October 2027 entry can apply through competitive applicant-led routes including:

  • NWSSDTP (ESRC) Studentships (Psychology pathway): Open to UK home and international applicants (the international fee difference is waived). Application deadline: 22 February 2027.
  • CSC–Lancaster Joint Scholarships: For citizens and permanent residents of China. Lancaster Stage 1 application deadline: 8 February 2027 (express interest early so we can prepare your application before the end of autumn term).
  • Self-funded or external scholarship routes: Also welcome year-round.

See our Join the Lab page for the step-by-step enquiry process, or email Bo Yao with your CV and a brief paragraph introducing your background and which project draws your interest.
